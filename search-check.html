Can people find The Stables at Wits End online?

We looked at your website the way Google and a first-time visitor do. Here is what we found, in plain English, with no jargon.

3 of 10

Your site passes 3 of the 10 basic checks. The place itself scores 10 out of 10 with guests, so the website is underselling it. All seven gaps are fixable.

The one that matters most

Your website is asking Google not to list it.

Every time a search engine visits your site, the site sends back a hidden instruction that means "do not show this page in search results". We tested it three times and got the same answer each time.

When we searched for "The Stables at Wits End", your own website did not come up. The results were booking sites such as Snaptrip, Vrbo and Sykes instead, and bookings through sites like those usually cost you commission.

On a website made with Canva this is usually one switch, called "Search engine visibility", that is turned off. It is the most likely cause here, though only you can see the setting to confirm it.

Six smaller things to fix

None of these is hard. Together they decide how you look in search results and when someone shares your link.

To fix

There is no description for Google to show

Under the blue link in a search result there are two lines of text. Your site does not supply any, so Google would have to guess.

A new site would say it for you: a converted stable for ten in Hartford, with a hot tub, games room and cinema room.

To fix

Shared links show no photograph

When a guest sends your link to their group on WhatsApp or Facebook, no picture or description is attached. For a place people book together, that share is your best advert.

A new site would attach your best photograph and a one-line description to every share.

To fix

The page has no main heading

Search engines read a page's headings to work out what it is about. The only heading on your page is "Terms and Support", which belongs to Canva, not to you.

A new site would have a clear heading for the house and for each part of it: sleeping, the hot tub, things to do, booking.

To fix

Eight of your ten pictures have no description

Each picture can carry a short written description. It helps your photographs appear in image searches, and it is read aloud to visitors who cannot see the screen.

A new site would describe every photograph.

To fix

Google is not told the basics about the business

Websites can hand Google a small fact sheet: what you are, where you are, how many you sleep, your phone number. Yours has none, so you miss out on the richer listings that show those details.

A new site would include that fact sheet, marked up as holiday accommodation in Hartford.

To fix

The site has no address of its own

Your address ends in "my.canva.site", which is Canva's free address. It is harder to remember, and Canva itself says free addresses cannot use Google's tools for site owners and take longer to be listed. There is also no map of the site for search engines to follow.

A new site would live at your own name, with everything search engines expect in place.

Can AI assistants find you?

More people now ask ChatGPT, Claude or Google's AI answers instead of searching. These tools read your website too. We checked four things they rely on. Your site passes 0 of 4.

To fix

AI assistants are allowed in

The same hidden instruction that keeps you out of Google also turns AI tools away.

To fix

The words can be read without running the page

Your page is built by a script after it loads. Without running it, a visitor sees about 15 words, and most AI tools do not run it.

To fix

Your phone number and address are written out

Neither appears in the words AI tools can read.

To fix

A summary file for AI tools

There is none. It is a newer idea, a short plain-text summary of who you are for AI tools, and it takes about twenty minutes to write.

At the moment an AI assistant asked about the Stables would most likely rely on Airbnb and booking sites rather than on you.

Nobody can make an AI recommend a business, and anyone who offers to is selling nothing. What a website can do is let these tools reach it, read it and get your facts right.

What we would do

You have a house guests rate 10 out of 10 and a website that search engines are told to ignore. A new site, at your own address, would fix all seven points above and give people a way to book with you directly.
